Signing tools end with a certificate beside the signed document: DocuSign's Certificate of Completion, Adobe Sign's Audit Report, Dropbox Sign's Audit Trail. Scrivr issues one with the executed PDF, from the same printing. Read this page before you rely on one: a certificate is evidence, and it is only as good as what you feed it and how you keep it.

<Callout type="warn">
Scrivr checks the document's own chain — the sealed snapshot, the executed PDF, the signatures it prints. It does **not** know who your signers are, how they proved it, or what happened in your app. Everything about people and events comes from you and is printed as your word, under your name. Treat the certificate as a record of your signing that Scrivr has checked against the document, not as proof of identity.
</Callout>

## What a certificate says

A certificate has two sections, and the difference between them is the whole point.

**Verified at issue** holds only what Scrivr checked in the call that issued it:

- the agreed content against its `contentHash` — the snapshot has not changed since it was sealed;
- the executed PDF's `artifactHash` — the hash of the document issued beside this certificate;
- each signature as that PDF prints it — a drawing it embedded (in its own proportions), or the typed words.

**Signing record — as recorded by** *your issuer* holds your word: the document's title and id, your parties and their details, when each signature was given, and your events. Scrivr checks that the record fits the document and time (see [What is checked](#what-is-checked)), and prints it as you gave it.

*Verified at issue* names no person as a fact. Each signature is shown as the executed PDF prints it — a typed signature's words, a drawing at the size it printed — headed by its kind and role ("Signature — client"), because which person holds that role is your fact, not Scrivr's. No email, IP address or signing time appears there.

Only signatures are shown — fields whose kind is a signature line. Initials, ticked boxes and every other answer are in the executed PDF, and they are covered by its hash, which the certificate does verify; they are not repeated on the certificate.

Your words can still reach that section in one way: its title and note are labels you may translate (see [Labels](#labels)). Scrivr never puts your record there; keeping your translated labels honest is yours.

## Issuing one

The executed PDF and its certificate come from **one call**, so a certificate can only ever describe the document beside it:

```ts
import { Field, StarterKit } from "@scrivr/core";
import { exportCompletionCertificate } from "@scrivr/export-pdf";

const result = await exportCompletionCertificate(sealed.snapshot, values, {
  // The setup the signers saw — Field with the same kinds and date format —
  // which prints the executed PDF; the certificate takes its fonts and page.
  editorOptions: { extensions: [StarterKit, Field.configure({ kinds })], fonts, pageConfig },
  record: {
    issuer: "Acme Sign",
    title: "Master Services Agreement",
    documentId: "AGR-2026-0042",
    parties: [
      { role: "client", name: "Ada Lovelace", email: "ada@example.com", authentication: "Email code" },
      { role: "provider", name: "Northwind Studio" },
      { role: "legal-cc", name: "Legal team" },            // a copy sent: no fields of its own
    ],
    events: [
      { at: "2026-11-15T08:00:00Z", kind: "sent", party: "client" },
      { at: "2026-11-15T08:00:00Z", kind: "sent", party: "provider" },
      { at: "2026-11-15T09:30:00Z", kind: "custom", label: "Identity verified", party: "client" },
      { at: "2026-11-15T12:44:10+03:00", kind: "signed", party: "client" }, // 09:44:10 UTC
      { at: "2026-11-15T10:02:00Z", kind: "signed", party: "provider" },
      { at: "2026-11-15T10:02:05Z", kind: "completed" },
    ],
  },
});

if (!result.ok) {
  showToSigner(result.refusal); // see What is checked
  return;
}

const { executed, certificate } = result;
// executed:    { pdf, artifactHash }
// certificate: { pdf, certificateHash, verified, content }
```

- `values` are every party's answers, as for [`exportExecutedPdf`](/docs/guides/fields#the-executed-document). Each signature's `signedAt` must be a full timestamp with its zone (see [Times](#times)), and its time must fit your events: a party is sent or views the document before it signs.
- `certificate.verified` is *Verified at issue* as data: `contentHash`, `artifactHash`, and each signature's `fieldId`, `role` and `kind`.
- `certificate.content` is the certificate as a document's JSON — open it in a read-only `Editor` to show it on screen as well as in print.
- Like the executed PDF, printing needs a browser `Editor`: a page, or a server with a headless DOM and a canvas. The certificate itself is printed in StarterKit with your `editorOptions`' fonts and page setup.

Do not build the executed PDF and the certificate in separate calls and pair them yourself. There is no API for that on purpose: nothing could prove the two belong together.

## What to keep

Store these together, where they cannot be changed afterwards:

| Keep | Why |
|---|---|
| The sealed snapshot (`content`, `contentHash`) | what was agreed; `verifySnapshot` proves it unchanged |
| `executed.pdf` and `executed.artifactHash` | the signed record, and the hash the certificate names |
| `certificate.pdf` and `certificate.certificateHash` | the certificate as issued |
| `certificate.verified` | what the certificate claims as checked, as data |
| The `values` you passed | every answer and its `signedAt` — what both PDFs were printed from, needed to check or issue them again |
| The `record` you passed, and your own evidence behind it | the certificate prints your record; your logs are what back it |

A hash proves bytes are the ones recorded only while the recorded hash is kept somewhere it cannot be quietly replaced — an append-only log, a database row written once, a timestamping service. If someone can change both the PDF and its stored hash, the hash proves nothing.

## The record

```ts
interface CompletionRecord {
  issuer: string;          // who keeps this record — printed as its source
  documentId?: string;     // your id for this envelope or agreement
  title?: string;
  parties: CompletionParty[];
  events: CompletionEvent[];
}

interface CompletionParty {
  role: string;            // the role its fields name, or one of your own for a party with no fields
  name: string;
  email?: string;
  authentication?: string; // how they proved who they are — "Email code", "SMS", "SSO"
  ip?: string;
  userAgent?: string;
}

type CompletionEvent =
  | { at: string; kind: "sent" | "viewed" | "consented" | "signed" | "declined" | "completed"; party?: string }
  | { at: string; kind: "custom"; label: string; party?: string };
```

- A detail you leave out — or leave blank — is not printed. A party with no name is headed by its role.
- **Personal data is only what you pass.** Scrivr reads no request, header or clock. Collect an IP address or user agent only if you have a reason and a lawful basis to — Adobe Sign, for one, leaves IP collection off unless an admin enables it.
- `custom` events are your own steps ("Identity verified", "Reminder sent"), printed by their label and placed by their time.
- When each signature was given comes from its value's `signedAt` — the stamp you put on it when you recorded it. It is printed beside its party in the record, as the signer gave it and in UTC: `2026-11-15 01:30:00 (UTC+03:00) · 2026-11-14 22:30:00 UTC`. It is your fact, not a checked one: with the default `formatSignedDate` the executed PDF prints only the day, in a date-signed field, from each party's latest signature — a `formatSignedDate` of your own may print more.
- One party per role, and every record names its `issuer` — a certificate never prints your facts under nobody's name.

## Times

Every time on a certificate — each event's `at`, each signature's `signedAt` — must be written `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm[:ss[.fff]]` followed by `Z` or `±hh:mm`, as `new Date().toISOString()` writes it, and name a day that exists:

- `2026-11-15T09:44:10Z`, `2026-11-15T09:44:10.250Z` and `2026-11-15T09:44:10+03:00` are accepted; seconds are optional, fractions are kept for ordering.
- `2026-11-15T09:44:10` (no zone) is refused: read on another machine it would name another moment.
- `2026-11-15` (a bare day) is refused for a certificate, though a field accepts it for its date: the certificate would have to invent the time.
- Other spellings — `+0300`, a lowercase `z`, a space for the `T`, `2026-02-30` — are refused too.

Events print in UTC, in time order — ties in the order you gave them — as DocuSign and Adobe Sign print theirs.

## What is checked

Before issuing, Scrivr checks the document, then your record against it and against time, in this order — the first that fails is the refusal.

| Check | Refusal |
|---|---|
| The snapshot matches its `contentHash` | `snapshot-altered` |
| Every required field is answered, as the executed PDF would print it — a drawing it cannot embed answers nothing | `unanswered-fields { missing }` |
| Every field's kind is one the editor setup knows — or it could not tell a signature from text | `unknown-field-kind { kind }` |
| The record names an issuer | `unnamed-issuer` |
| Every printed signature carries a `signedAt`, written as [Times](#times) says | `undated-signature { fieldId }`, `unzoned-time { at }` |
| Every event is one of the six kinds, or a `custom` step with a label, at a time written as [Times](#times) says | `unknown-event { event }`, `unzoned-time { at }` |
| One party per role | `duplicate-party { role }` |
| Every role that answered or must answer a field has a party | `missing-party { role }` |
| Every event's `party`, when it names one, is one of your parties | `unknown-party { role }` |
| A party first comes to the document — its first `sent`, `viewed` or `consented` — before its first signing, whether that is a printed signature or your `signed` event | `events-out-of-order { event }` |
| No signature is dated after `completed` | `signed-after-completed { fieldId }` |
| No `signed` event follows `completed` | `events-out-of-order { event }` |

What passes, on purpose, because signing tools record it: a party viewing the document again after signing, or after completion; a party with no fields of its own; a party with no `sent` or `viewed` event at all. `declined` is labelled, not checked: whether a declined party may still complete is your rule.

A refusal is an answer, not an exception — show it to whoever can fix it:

```ts
const words: Record<string, string> = {
  "snapshot-altered": "The stored agreement no longer matches what was sealed.",
  "unanswered-fields": "Some required fields are still empty.",
  "unknown-field-kind": "A field is of a kind this app does not know.",
  "unnamed-issuer": "The record does not say who issued it.",
  "duplicate-party": "Two people are recorded for the same role.",
  "unknown-event": "The record holds an event that cannot be named.",
  "missing-party": "A party who signed is missing from the record.",
  "unknown-party": "The record names a party who is not part of this signing.",
  "unzoned-time": "A time is not written as a full time with its zone.",
  "undated-signature": "A signature was recorded without a time.",
  "signed-after-completed": "A signature is dated after the signing completed.",
  "events-out-of-order": "The record's events are out of order.",
};
```

## Making it yours

### Labels

Every fixed title and label comes from one table, so you can issue a certificate in your signers' language. Pass only what you change; a label you leave out, or blank, stays English:

```ts
labels: {
  title: "Certificat d'achèvement",
  verified: "Vérifié à l'émission",
  record: "Dossier de signature — tel qu'enregistré par",
  eventKinds: { sent: "Envoyé", signed: "Signé", completed: "Terminé" },
},
```

`DEFAULT_CERTIFICATE_LABELS` holds the English defaults — spread it as the base for a full translation. A signature is headed by its field kind's own label (from `Field`'s kinds) — both under *Verified at issue* and beside its time in the record ("Signature: 2026-11-15 …") — and a party by its name.

Translating `verified` or `verifiedNote` is fine. Rewording them to claim more than Scrivr checked is not: they are printed inside *Verified at issue*, and the note says what was checked so a reader can rely on it. Scrivr cannot tell a translation from a new claim, so this one is yours to keep honest.

### Your own content

`content` lets you add your own blocks around the certificate, or lay out the record your own way. It is handed what Scrivr would print and the facts behind it (`CertificateParts`), and returns your blocks (`CertificateContent`):

```ts
import { exportCompletionCertificate, type CertificateContent, type CertificateParts } from "@scrivr/export-pdf";
import type { DocumentBlock } from "@scrivr/core";

const content = ({ record, labels }: CertificateParts): CertificateContent => ({
  // Above the certificate's title: your logo, an introduction.
  before: [{ image: logoDataUrl, width: 120, height: 40 }, { paragraph: "Acme Sign — completion record" }],
  // The record's body, your way: here Scrivr's own, less the IP addresses —
  // found by the label they are printed under, your translation if you gave one.
  record: record.filter((block: DocumentBlock) => !("label" in block && block.label === labels.ip)),
  // At the end: your disclosure, consent text, support contact.
  after: [
    { title: "Electronic record and signature disclosure", level: 3 },
    { paragraph: disclosureText },
  ],
});

const result = await exportCompletionCertificate(snapshot, values, { editorOptions, record: signingRecord, content });
```

`CertificateParts` hands you `record`, the `labels` it is printed under (yours where you gave them), `verified` (the checked facts) and `signingRecord` (your record as you gave it), to lay the record out from scratch. `verified` and `labels` are frozen and typed read-only.

- Blocks are `DocumentBlock`s from `@scrivr/core`: `{ title, level: 1 | 2 | 3 }`, `{ paragraph }`, `{ label, value }` (a bold label and its value) and `{ image, width, height }`. An image is fetched like any image in an export — prefer an inline PNG or JPEG `data:` URL, so the certificate depends on nothing at a URL.
- `record` is the body of the record section as Scrivr lays it out — keep it, filter it, add to it, or replace it with your own built from `signingRecord`. Leave out any of `before`, `record`, `after` and that part stays Scrivr's.
- **What you cannot change:** what *Verified at issue* holds, and the record's title naming your issuer. The section is laid out before `content` is called, and `verified` and `labels` are handed to you frozen, so your content can sit around what was checked and never inside it. The checks run on your `record` data whatever you print, so content cannot print its way past a refusal.

Your content is yours to keep honest. Scrivr cannot stop a block that *says* "verified", so do not word your own content as if Scrivr had checked it.

## One file

Adobe Sign and Documenso append the certificate to the signed document. To deliver one file, bundle the two:

```ts
import { bundleCertificate } from "@scrivr/export-pdf";

const { pdf, bundleHash } = await bundleCertificate(executed.pdf, certificate.pdf);
```

The bundle is the executed pages, then the certificate's, under a `bundleHash` of its own. Keep the executed PDF and its `artifactHash` too: the certificate inside the bundle names the executed PDF's hash, which is the hash of that file alone, not of the bundle.

## What it does not do

- **It is not a digital signature.** Scrivr returns bytes and their hash. To seal the executed PDF, the certificate or the bundle with an X.509 certificate (PAdES), so a PDF reader shows it as signed and any change breaks it, sign the bytes with your own key and signing service. Your key never enters Scrivr.
- **It does not identify anyone.** Authentication, consent screens and identity checks are yours. Record what you did in `authentication`, `custom` events and your own logs.
- **It does not check your events against your logs.** Order and zones are checked; whether an event happened is your record.
- **It is not issued mid-signing.** A certificate is issued only once every required field is answered. There is no interim report.
- **It does not store anything.** Where and how you keep the PDFs, hashes and evidence is yours — see [What to keep](#what-to-keep).
